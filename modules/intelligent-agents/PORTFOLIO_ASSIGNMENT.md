# Portfolio Assignment Brief

The final assessment in this module is an individual e-portfolio that collates all the evidence of your work in this module. It accounts for 40% of the overall module mark. The word count for the reflective section is 1000 words and the rest of your individual e-portfolio has a word count of up to 1500 words. The total word count for this assignment is 2500 words.

Guidance on the use of AI, particularly within summative assessments, can be found in the Student Information area.

Assignment Details
As a learning and development e-portfolio, specific requirements for what should be included are detailed in the “Module E-Portfolio Learning Activities” section. Elaborating on these requirements, your e-portfolio should present a coherent and reflective narrative of your learning, development, and applied practice throughout the module.

Your e-portfolio should consist of:

Your answers to and outputs from exercises carried out during the module, including all coding scripts, agent implementations, and system designs.
Your analysis of data gathered and your contributions to online forums, demonstrating analytical and critical thinking skills.
Reflective commentary on intelligent agents, focusing on the skills, knowledge, and understanding you have developed during the module.
A critical evaluation of your final project, reflecting on both the design phase (week 7) and the implemented solution (week 11).
In addition to the above, your e-portfolio should include three formative reflective case studies. These case studies should be based on themes explored during the module and should relate directly to your practical work, design decisions, or observations made while developing and testing agent-based systems.

You should select three of the following themes and develop each one as a short reflective case study:

Bias in autonomous decision-making.
AI safety constraints (e.g. kill switches, bounded autonomy, safe exploration).
Explainable decisions in agents (XAI).
Legal and ethical frameworks for autonomous systems.
Responsible deployment of agents, including avoiding harmful or unintended emergent behaviours.
Each case study should be grounded in your own experience on the module and linked to the systems you designed or implemented. You are encouraged to critically reflect on what occurred, why it matters, and how it informs your future practice as a developer of intelligent agents.

Reflective Format
You may present your reflective content either as written text or as a video recording with comprehensive English subtitles. In developing your reflections, a diary-style approach may be helpful. You are encouraged to structure your reflections using Rolfe et al.’s (2001) reflective framework:

WHAT – a brief description of the project outcome, technical decision, or critical incident.
SO WHAT – analysis and interpretation of its significance, including technical, ethical, or professional implications.
NOW WHAT – reflection on how this learning will influence your future development or professional practice.
If submitting a video reflection, this may consist of a series of short video logs recorded throughout the module and edited together into a single, coherent reflective narrative for final submission. The video must flow as an integrated account of your experience and include clear, accurate subtitles.

Team Project Brief

This assignment has two component parts, the team submission and your individual peer assessment. All components must be submitted by end of Unit 6. This accounts for 20% of your final module mark. Only one submission is required from each team. Nominate one member of your team to submit on the team's behalf. The word count is 1,000 words.

Guidance on the use of AI, particularly within summative assessments, can be found in the Student Information area.

Full Brief
This assignment sees you positioned as a team of software consultants and specialists in agent design and development.

You are required to design and develop an LLM-powered planning agent capable of performing tasks for an organisation with domain-specific requirements. A domain refers to a group of users with similar application and hardware requirements. A domain may be characterised by having:

A need to find certain files, documents, or online content.
The need to carry out some processing or reasoning over that data.
A requirement to do something meaningful with the processed data.
The requirements mean that one or more agents may need to work together to carry out these operations. In this context, agents may have different responsibilities, such as planning, data retrieval, processing, or storage, and cooperate to achieve an overall goal.

The task area you should focus on for this assignment is:

Academic research and information gathering using an LLM-powered planning agent.

In this domain, the agent receives a high-level goal (e.g. a research topic or query), decomposes it into subtasks, retrieves relevant information from online sources (such as websites or search engines), processes the retrieved data (e.g. filtering, summarisation, ranking), and sends or stores the results in an offline location for later use.

You should design your system so that one or more small programs (agents) operate based on their own goals, intentions, or roles, with the LLM used to support planning, reasoning, or task decomposition. The system should be feasible to implement fully or partially using available technologies, including free-tier services such as Hugging Face models and other open-source or cloud-based tools (e.g. Azure free tiers).

Success Criteria
The agreed success criteria for successful development are that:

The agent can identify and retrieve relevant data.
The data is processed in some way (e.g. structured, summarised, or filtered).
The processed information is stored, saved, or presented in a usable format.
The Design Proposal Document

Your team is expected to prepare and deliver a design proposal report of your intended development work for the organisation. Note that the associated grading criteria are highlighted in the requirements below and should be reviewed alongside the criteria grid (Module Resources).

Your report should cover:

The system requirements, for example which libraries, models, APIs, or platforms are required (including any LLMs or cloud services).
The design decisions, explaining why particular agent architectures, planning approaches, or technologies were chosen.
The approach you have adopted to create your system, such as the development methodology used and the agent-oriented techniques being leveraged.
A clear rationale supported by academic literature, explaining and justifying your choices with appropriate references (Knowledge and Understanding weighted at 25%).
The report should also identify the challenges you expect to encounter when developing your agent. For example, challenges may include task decomposition, unreliable or noisy online data, explainability of LLM-based decisions, or managing cooperation between agents. You should briefly indicate which paradigms, patterns, theories, or practices you intend to use to address these challenges and justify your approaches with reference to the literature (Application and Understanding weighted at 25%).

You must produce a minimum of two different graphical designs illustrating different views of the system. These may include UML-based representations such as class diagrams, sequence diagrams, and/or activity diagrams, or other appropriate visual models.

You should also clearly state all tools, libraries, models, and development environments you intend to use in your solution (Criticality weighted at 25%).

Presentation and Structure
Presentation and structure of your work (weighted at 25%) includes spelling, academic writing style, evidence of proofreading, and correct use and formatting of citations and references.

Appendices should not be used to extend the core report. Reports should stand alone as complete and concise documents. Appendices should only be used where genuinely necessary for supplementary or supporting information. One key aim of this assessment is to demonstrate the ability to express ideas succinctly and clearly.

Checklist for the Assignment
Your submission should include:

A bulleted list of system requirements and assumptions, design decisions, and approaches, supported by academic research and references.
A bulleted list of anticipated challenges.
Graphical system designs using multiple diagrams (e.g. class, sequence, activity).
A bulleted list of tools, development and testing environments, libraries, and models to be used.
Evidence of spelling and grammar checking and proofreading.
